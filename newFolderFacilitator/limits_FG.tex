\textbf{Student Learning Outcomes}
Students will be able to:
  \begin{itemize}   
    \item Evaluate one- and two-sided limits using a graph.
    \item Identify when a limit does not exist. 
    \item Sketch a graph based on limit and function value information.
    \item (Challenge) Write an equation for a function based on limit and function value information.
  \end{itemize}

\textbf{Prerequisite Knowledge:} This activity expects that students will be familiar with limits on a graph, but not the limit laws or other methods for finding limits.  \\
\\

This activity seems to either go very quickly or expose groups' misunderstanding of limits. Encourage students to dig deeper into what a limit is/is not, as this will be the foundation of all the work we do this semester (and into Calc II).  The challenge problem at the end is \textit{more} than we would ask students on an assement, generally, but provides something for groups who finish quickly to dig into.

\begin{exercise}   
  Consider the piecewise function 
  \[f(x)=
  \begin{cases}
    3x-7 & x\leq 0\\
    x^2+1 & 0<x<2\\
    4x-3 & x\geq 2
  \end{cases}\]
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Sketch a graph of the function.
    \item Evaluate $\lim_{x\rightarrow 0^-}f(x)$.
    \textcolor{blue}{$=-7$}
    \item Evaluate $\lim_{x\rightarrow 0^+}f(x)$.
    \textcolor{blue}{$=1$}
    \item Evaluate $\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}f(x)$.
    \textcolor{blue}{does not exist (jump)}
    \item Evaluate $\lim_{x\rightarrow 2^-}f(x)$.
    \textcolor{blue}{$=5$}
    \item Evaluate $\lim_{x\rightarrow 2^+}f(x)$.
    \textcolor{blue}{$=5$}
    \item Evaluate $\lim_{x\rightarrow 2}f(x)$.
    \textcolor{blue}{$=5$}
  \end{enumerate}
  \textcolor{orange}{The intention here is to first, help reinforce how to graph a piecewise function, and also to help students understand how limits and piecewise functions fit together.}
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}
    \textbf{Challenge Problem} Write an \textit{equation} of a function, $f$, that satisfies the following conditions.  (Hint: A piecewise function may be helpful here.)
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow -2^-}f(x)=-2$
        \item $\displaystyle \lim_{x\rightarrow -2^+}f(x)=3$
        \item $\displaystyle \lim_{x\rightarrow 1}f(x)=0$
        \item $f(-2)=3$
        \item $f(1)\neq 0$
    \end{enumerate}

    \textcolor{blue}{For example, \[f(x)=\begin{cases}
    x & x<-2\\
    -x+1 & -2\leq x<1\\
    -4 & x=1\\
    x-1 & x>1
    \end{cases}\]}
    \textcolor{blue}{is a simple answer (the function need not change at $x=1$, as long as a separate point is defined at $x=1$).  Many other examples are possible!}

    \textcolor{orange}{This truly is meant to be a challenge problem.  If multiple groups get to it and still have extra time, have the groups swap answers and check each others' work.}
\end{exercise}
